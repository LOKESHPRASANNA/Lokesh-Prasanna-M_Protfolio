01. Overview

Project Overview

The Smart Shopping Trolley is an IoT-integrated retail solution that uses RFID sensors to automatically scan products as they're placed in the trolley. The UI/UX design focused on creating an intuitive, stress-free shopping and self-checkout experience. This project was published as a research paper at an IEEE Conference.

02. Problem Statement

The Problem

"Long billing queues, poor product discoverability, and the tedious checkout experience create significant friction in supermarket shopping, reducing customer satisfaction and store efficiency."

Traditional supermarket checkout requires customers to wait in long queues, manually scan products, and interact with complex billing systems. This creates a bottleneck that frustrates customers and reduces store throughput. The IoT-based smart trolley eliminates this by enabling real-time scanning as products are added.

😤

Key Pain Points

  • Average 15-minute checkout queue wait times
  • No real-time spending visibility while shopping
  • Difficult to find products in large stores
  • Complicated self-checkout kiosk interfaces
  • No personalized recommendations or deals
🎯

Design Objectives

  • Display real-time cart total as products are added
  • Create a 3-step self-checkout flow
  • Provide in-store navigation and product search
  • Design large touch targets for in-trolley display
  • Support multiple payment methods seamlessly
03. Research

User Research & Competitive Analysis

🛒

Field Research

Observed 50+ shoppers in a local supermarket, timing their checkout experience and documenting frustration points throughout the shopping journey.

📱

App Analysis

Evaluated Amazon Go, Walmart Scan & Go, and local retail apps. Identified UX gaps in product scanning feedback and payment flow simplicity.

🔬

IEEE Research

Reviewed 12 existing IoT retail papers to understand technical constraints and design opportunities unique to smart trolley form factors.

👤

User Persona — Meena, 35

Working professional, weekly grocery shopper

Time-constrained, shops with a budget in mind. Frustrated by unexpected total at checkout. Wants to know what she's spending in real-time.

User Journey Map

Enter store → Grab trolley → Browse aisles → Add items (auto-scan) → Check running total on display → Navigate to payment → Tap to pay → Receipt → Exit

Key opportunity: Eliminate the dedicated checkout counter entirely for a frictionless exit experience.

Core UX Principles Applied

👁️

Glanceability

All key information (cart total, item count) visible in 2 seconds without interaction. Large fonts, high contrast for in-store lighting conditions.

👆

Touch-Friendly

Minimum 48px touch targets throughout. Large, thumb-friendly buttons optimized for use while pushing a shopping trolley.

⚡

Speed

Maximum 3 taps to complete payment. Instant visual feedback on product scan. No unnecessary confirmation dialogs.

08. Reflection

Lessons Learned

🔧

Design for Constraints

Designing for an IoT device with a fixed screen size and physical context (supermarket environment, wet hands) forced creative solutions for interaction design.

📐

Physical Context Matters

UI that works on a phone screen doesn't automatically work on a trolley-mounted display. Real-world testing in the actual environment was essential.

🤝

Cross-discipline Collaboration

Working closely with hardware engineers gave me invaluable insight into technical constraints and how design decisions directly impact implementation.
